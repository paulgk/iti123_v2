\section{Introduction}

I play badminton recreationally. Getting technique feedback without a coach is hard. This project tests whether AI can classify two overhead shots using only body pose data:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Clear}: Hit shuttlecock high and deep
\item \textbf{Smash}: Attack shot going steeply downward
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Research question}: Can skeletal keypoint data from video distinguish these strokes?

No racket tracking. No shuttlecock tracking. Just body pose.

\subsection{Dataset}

I used ShuttleSet---video clips from professional matches.

\noindent Numbers:
\begin{itemize}
\item Started with 4,983 clips
\item Removed 301 backhand clips
\item 4,682 forehand clips (2,371 Clear, 2,311 Smash)
\item MediaPipe extracted poses from 99.4\% (4,655 successful)
\item From 40 different matches
\end{itemize}

Class balance: 50.6\% Clear, 49.4\% Smash.

\subsection{Pipeline}

Six steps:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Pose extraction using MediaPipe
\item Feature engineering (60 features per frame)
\item Group-stratified data splitting
\item Model training (multiple architectures)
\item Statistical validation
\item Results analysis
\end{enumerate}

\section{Technical Implementation}

\subsection{Pose Extraction}

MediaPipe Pose extracts 33 body keypoints per frame with $x$, $y$, $z$ coordinates. I configured it for highest accuracy (model complexity 2).

Key landmarks: shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, ankles, nose.

\subsection{Feature Engineering}

Raw coordinates aren't enough. I created 60 features per frame:

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Feature Categories}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Category & Description \\
\midrule
Arm Extension & Distance from shoulder to wrist \\
Depth & Wrist/elbow depth relative to shoulder/hip \\
Height & Wrist/elbow height relative to shoulder/head \\
Joint Angles & Elbow angle, shoulder angle \\
Body Posture & Torso lean, shoulder rotation \\
Velocities & First derivative of all spatial features \\
\addlinespace
\multicolumn{2}{l}{\textbf{Wrist/Forearm Features (9 features):}} \\
\quad r forearm vertical angle & Angle between forearm and vertical \\
\quad r wrist elbow vertical & Wrist height above elbow \\
\quad r forearm pitch & Forearm elevation angle \\
\quad + 6 more & Pronation, horizontal reach, arm plane angles \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\textbf{Hypothesis}: Wrist angle would discriminate strokes. Clear uses extended wrist (120--150$^\circ$). Smash uses flexed wrist (30--60$^\circ$).

\subsection{Data Splitting}

Random splitting causes leakage. I used group-stratified splitting---all clips from same match stay together.

Results:

\subsection{Padding and Normalization}

Videos have different lengths. I padded to 50 frames. To avoid corrupting normalization, I computed statistics from real frames only, then padded afterward.

Models use Masking layers to ignore padding during training.

\subsection{Model Architectures}

I tested four baseline models:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{ResNet1D}: Pattern detector across motion sequence
\item \textbf{LSTM}: Memory-based, remembers motion history
\item \textbf{BiLSTM}: Processes sequence forward and backward
\item \textbf{GRU}: Simpler, faster version of LSTM
\end{itemize}

Training config:
\begin{itemize}
\item Optimizer: Adam (lr 0.001)
\item Loss: Binary crossentropy
\item Batch size: 32
\item Max epochs: 50, early stopping (patience 10)
\end{itemize}

All models take input: 50 timesteps $\times$ 60 features.

\section{Analysis}

\subsection{Why Classification Failed}

Dataset captures shuttlecock contact moment only. At this instant, clear and smash have identical body poses:

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Body Position at Contact}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Feature & Clear & Smash \\
\midrule
Racket position & Overhead & Overhead \\
Contact height & 2m above court & 2m above court \\
Arm extension & Fully extended & Fully extended \\
Body posture & Upright & Upright \\
Forearm angle & 85.3$^\circ$ $\pm$ 45.2$^\circ$ & 84.2$^\circ$ $\pm$ 44.8$^\circ$ \\
\midrule
Difference & \multicolumn{2}{c}{1.1$^\circ$ (negligible)} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

My wrist angle hypothesis was wrong. Expected 60--90$^\circ$ difference. Got 1.1$^\circ$.

\subsection{Missing Information}

Key discriminators aren't in pose data:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Racket face angle}: MediaPipe doesn't track equipment. Clear angles racket upward. Smash angles downward. Most important feature---missing.
\item \textbf{Shuttlecock trajectory}: Clears go high. Smashes go steep. Clips end at contact. No trajectory.
\item \textbf{Follow-through}: Clears continue upward. Smashes snap downward. Clips end at contact. No follow-through.
\item \textbf{Racket velocity}: Smashes have higher speeds. Body velocities don't capture this.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What Worked}

Despite failure, methodology was sound:
\begin{itemize}
\item Group-stratified splitting prevented data leakage
\item Proper padding and normalization maintained data integrity
\item Multiple architectures validated conclusion
\item Early failure prevented wasted effort on model tuning
\end{itemize}

\section{Recommendations}

\subsection{In the next phase}

Four approaches to make this work:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Different strokes}: Add different stroke like lift or backhand to improve classification 
\item \textbf{Improve Feature engineering}: Add more feature engineering ideas from coaching perspective that can detect the shot
\item \textbf{Extend time window}: Include 2--3 seconds after contact. Capture follow-through. Medium impact.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}

This project tested whether body pose alone distinguishes badminton clear and smash strokes.

\textbf{Answer}: No.

\textbf{Key finding}: Clear and smash have identical body poses at contact. Difference: 1.1$^\circ$ in forearm angle. Models achieved 50\% accuracy---equivalent to random guessing.

What I built:
\begin{itemize}
\item Complete ML pipeline: Raw match videos to clip extraction and pose extraction
\item 60 engineered features including 9 domain-specific wrist features
\item Proper data handling: group-stratified split, padding masking
\item Multiple architectures: baselines to Transformers
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Next steps}: Success requires (1) Different model evaluation and (2) different stroke pairs with distinct body mechanics.
